# Lesson 11 — Story: Crassus et Carbō

Vocabulary: Knudsvig chapters 1–11 (dataset `knudsvig-731`). Grammar through Lesson 11:
3rd-person verbs only, present and perfect tenses, active and passive; nominative, accusative,
and ablative cases (ablative with or without a preposition); adjectives, numerals, *is ea id*,
*hic haec hoc*, *ille illa illud*; dependent clauses with the indicative (*cum, dum, postquam,
quia, quod, sī, ubi, ut*). Not yet: dative, genitive, accusative with prepositions, 1st/2nd
person, imperfect/future, subjunctive.

A ten-sentence story about two brothers, a wolf, and thin ice. Nine sentences have a dependent
clause; the kernel types are mixed (existential ×2, transitive, intransitive, linking, passive,
factitive, complementary infinitive). The tags in the English list name the kernel of the main
clause and the dependent clause.

## Lesson 11

### Latin

in agrō antīquō fuērunt multa animālia et duo frātrēs, Crassus et Carbō. quod Crassus multum vīnum amāvit, ā populō crassus appellātus est. Carbō autem parvus sed sapiēns fuit, quia multās rēs ā medicō didicit. ūnā nocte, dum frātrēs dormiunt, lupus agnum ex agrō rapuit. ubi Carbō hoc vīdit, frātrem excitāvit. postquam Crassus excitātus est, lupum cum agnō in glaciē vīdit et eum capere voluit. sed in eō locō fuit glaciēs fragilis, et Carbō ait: "sī homō crassus in hāc glaciē ambulat, glaciēs frangitur." cum Crassus in glaciē ambulāvit, glaciēs corpore crassō frācta est. ut lupus in aquā periit, Carbō frātrem et agnum magnā cum cūrā ex aquā rapuit. ex eō tempore populus Carbōnem, quod frātrem fortiter adiūvit, nōn parvum sed magnum appellāvit.

### English

1. In an old field there were many animals and two brothers, Crassus and Carbo. *(existential, verb before subject — no dependent clause)*
2. Because Crassus loved a lot of wine, he was called "Fat" by the people. *(passive of a factitive verb, agent ā populō; nom. predicate crassus — quod: cause)*
3. Carbo, however, was small but wise, because he had learned many things from a physician. *(linking, two predicate adjectives — quia: cause)*
4. One night, while the brothers were sleeping, a wolf snatched a lamb from the field. *(transitive; ūnā nocte time — dum + present with a perfect main verb)*
5. When Carbo saw this, he woke his brother. *(transitive — ubi: time)*
6. After Crassus was woken, he saw the wolf with the lamb on the ice and wanted to catch it. *(transitive + complementary infinitive — postquam: time, passive verb)*
7. But in that place there was fragile ice, and Carbo says: "If a fat man walks on this ice, the ice is broken." *(existential, verb before subject; fragilis is attributive, not a predicate — sī: condition, present general; passive frangitur)*
8. When Crassus walked on the ice, the ice was broken by his fat body. *(passive, ablative of means corpore crassō — cum: time; intransitive verb in the clause)*
9. When the wolf died in the water, Carbo pulled his brother and the lamb out of the water with great care. *(transitive; manner magnā cum cūrā — ut: time, intransitive verb in the clause)*
10. From that time on, the people called Carbo not small but great, because he had bravely helped his brother. *(factitive, object complements parvum / magnum — quod: cause, placed inside the main clause)*
