# Lesson 10 — Kernel Discrimination and Demonstrative Forms

Vocabulary: Knudsvig chapters 1–10 (dataset `knudsvig-731`). Grammar through Lesson 10:
3rd-person verbs only, present and perfect tenses, active and passive; nominative, accusative,
and ablative cases (ablative with or without a preposition); adjectives, numerals, *is ea id*,
*hic haec hoc*, *ille illa illud*. Not yet: dative, genitive, accusative with prepositions,
1st/2nd person, imperfect/future, subjunctive.

Ten sentences, two of each kernel type (copular, factitive, transitive, passive, existential),
in mixed order. Each one carries a trap that points toward a wrong type: an attributive
adjective that looks like an object complement, a passive factitive that looks copular, a
predicate placed before its subject, a verb gapped or placed first. The tags in the English
list give the answer and name the trap.

## Lesson 10

### Latin

1. **ille puer ā populō dux appellātus est.**
2. **medicus timidum puerum magnā cum cūrā cūrāvit.**
3. **nōn semper fēlīx est sapiēns.**
4. **hunc morbum longum medicī brevem appellāvērunt.**
5. **eō tempore in castrīs fuērunt duo medicī et multī morbī.**
6. **hanc fēminam sapientem multī virī amāvērunt, sed illa eōs nōn amāvit.**
7. **in perīculō timidus saepe fit etiam fortis vir.**
8. **aciēs eō tempore ā Caesare in agrō īnstrūcta est.**
9. **ducem eum fēcit nōn fortūna sed sapientia.**
10. **in hāc vītā nōn est fēlīcitās sine cūrā.**

### English

1. That boy was called leader by the people. *(passive — trap: two nominatives (puer, dux) look copular; it is the passive of a factitive verb, with agent ā populō)*
2. The physician cared for the timid boy with great care. *(transitive — trap: timidum is attributive, not an object complement; cūrō is not a verb of naming/making)*
3. The wise man is not always fortunate. *(copular — trap: predicate adj. and verb come before the subject; both words are adjectives, sapiēns used as a noun)*
4. The physicians called this long disease short. *(factitive — trap: two accusative adjectives; longum is attributive, brevem is the object complement)*
5. At that time there were two physicians and many diseases in the camp. *(existential — trap: plural fuērunt with nominatives but no predicate; eō tempore ablative of time)*
6. Many men loved this wise woman, but she did not love them. *(transitive ×2 — trap: object first and sapientem looks like a complement, but amō is not factitive)*
7. In danger even a brave man often becomes timid. *(copular: fīō — trap: predicate timidus placed before the verb, subject fortis vir at the end; fit is not passive here)*
8. At that time the battle line was drawn up by Caesar in the field. *(passive — trap: īnstrūcta est looks like adjective + est; agent ā Caesare)*
9. Not fortune but wisdom made him leader. *(factitive: faciō — trap: object and complement first, subjects last; fēcit is active, not copular)*
10. In this life there is no happiness without care. *(existential — trap: nōn est + nominative with no predicate; sine cūrā is adverbial)*

## Forms: *is*, *ille*, *hic* (nominative, accusative, ablative)

### Questions

1. What is the nom. sg. f. of *hic*?
2. What is the acc. sg. n. of *hic*?
3. What is the nom. pl. n. of *hic*?
4. What is the abl. sg. f. of *hic*?
5. What is the nom. pl. n. of *ille*?
6. What is the acc. pl. f. of *ille*?
7. What is the abl. sg. m. of *ille*?
8. What is the acc. sg. f. of *is*?
9. What is the nom. pl. m. of *is*?
10. What is the abl. pl. n. of *is*?

### Answers

1. haec
2. hoc
3. haec *(same as nom. sg. f.)*
4. hāc
5. illa *(same as nom. sg. f., but short final -a)*
6. illās
7. illō
8. eam
9. eī / iī
10. eīs / iīs *(same for all genders)*
