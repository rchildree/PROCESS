# Lesson 11 — Story: Gladiātor et Aper

Vocabulary: Knudsvig chapters 1–11 (dataset `knudsvig-731`). Grammar through Lesson 11:
3rd-person verbs only, present and perfect tenses, active and passive; nominative, accusative,
and ablative cases (ablative with or without a preposition); adjectives, numerals, *is ea id*,
*hic haec hoc*, *ille illa illud*; dependent clauses with the indicative (*cum, dum, postquam,
quia, quod, sī, ubi, ut*). Not yet: dative, genitive, accusative with prepositions, 1st/2nd
person, imperfect/future, subjunctive.

A ten-sentence story about a timid young gladiator, a boar, and a handful of sand. Nine
sentences have a dependent clause, and all eight subordinating conjunctions appear (*ut* twice,
once as "as" and once as "when"); the kernel types are mixed (existential ×2, passive ×2,
transitive ×2, linking, intransitive, factitive, complementary infinitive). The tags in the
English list name the kernel of the main clause and the dependent clause.

## Lesson 11

### Latin

ūnō diē in harēnā fuērunt mīlle hominēs et Caesar cum senātū. quia populus novum mūnus vidēre cupīvit, gladiātor parvus ā Caesare prōductus est. hic gladiātor, ut omnēs hominēs cognōvērunt, nōn vir fortis sed timidus fuit. ubi aper magnus et crūdēlis harēnam intrāvit, hic gladiātor sine morā fūgit. dum fugit, in multitūdine fuit magna īra. cum Caesar hoc vīdit, gladiātōrem nōn virum sed vulpem timidam vocāvit. postquam gladiātor illud audīvit, nōn iam fugere sed aprum vincere voluit. ut aper magnō impetū vēnit, gladiātor eum harēnā impedīvit. quod aper gladiātōrem vidēre nōn potuit, ab eō facile interfectus est. tandem Caesar gladiātōrem corōnāvit et ait: “sī vir sapiēns est, etiam aprum crūdēlem vincere potest.”

### English

1. One day in the arena there were a thousand people and Caesar with the senate. *(existential, verb before subject; ūnō diē time — no dependent clause)*
2. Because the people wanted to see a new show, a small gladiator was brought out by Caesar. *(passive, agent ā Caesare — quia: cause, placed first; complementary infinitive vidēre in the clause)*
3. This gladiator, as everyone knew, was not a brave man but a timid boy. *(linking, predicate nouns with adjectives vir fortis / puer timidus — ut: "as," placed inside the main clause)*
4. When a large and cruel boar entered the arena, the gladiator fled without delay. *(intransitive; manner sine morā — ubi: time, transitive verb in the clause)*
5. While the gladiator was fleeing, there was great anger in the crowd. *(existential, verb before subject — dum + present with a perfect main verb)*
6. When Caesar saw this, he called the gladiator not a man but a timid fox. *(factitive, object complements virum / vulpem timidam — cum: time)*
7. After the gladiator heard that, he no longer wanted to flee but to conquer the boar. *(complementary infinitives fugere / vincere; nōn iam "no longer" — postquam: time)*
8. When the boar came at him with a great charge, the gladiator hindered it with sand. *(transitive; means harēnā; manner magnō impetū in the clause — ut: "when," intransitive verb in the clause)*
9. Because the boar could not see the gladiator, it was easily killed by him. *(passive, agent ab eō — quod: cause; complementary infinitive vidēre in the clause)*
10. At last Caesar crowned the gladiator and says: "If a boy is wise, he conquers even a cruel boar." *(transitive, compound with ait — sī: condition, present general; linking verb in the clause)*
