# Lesson 15 — Story: Agrippīna — Key

Story: `lesson-15-story-agrippina.md`. This key follows the Latin paragraph as it now stands,
split into the same 17 sentences as the English list.

Markup: participial clauses that modify a noun in the main clause (Lesson 14) are in
**[square brackets]**; ablative absolutes (Lesson 15) are in **{braces}**. Both are
participial clauses. The difference is the noun the participle goes with: in a [bracketed]
clause it has a job in the main clause (subject, object, agent…); in a {braced} clause the
ablative noun is the clause's own subject and has no job outside it.

For each ablative absolute the key gives two versions, as in the Lesson 15 notes:
**absolute**, an English absolute phrase that adds nothing; and **clause**, a full
subordinate clause with a conjunction chosen from the context (time, cause, concession,
condition).

## Sentence by sentence

**1.** {Claudiō Rōmam regente}, Agrippīna, fēmina [magnā avāritiā affecta], cum eō in urbe vīxit.

- While Claudius was ruling Rome, Agrippina, a woman gripped by great greed, lived with him in the city.
- Main kernel: intransitive (*vīxit*). *cum eō*: accompaniment; *eō* is m. abl. sg. of *is* (Claudius), not the verb "I go."
- **{Claudiō … regente}**: pres. act., m. abl. sg. The participle has its own object, *Rōmam*. Absolute: "Claudius ruling Rome." Clause: "while Claudius was ruling Rome" (time, at the same time as the main verb).
- **[affecta]**: pf. pass., f. nom. sg., modifying *fēmina*, which is in apposition to the subject *Agrippīna*. *magnā avāritiā*: ablative of means with a passive participle. Description: "a woman (who was) gripped by great greed."
- *Claudiō* looks like a dative, but there is no dative yet: it is ablative, the subject of the ablative absolute.

**2.** Agrippīna, [Nerōnem puerum suum plūrimum amāns], vīrēs magnās per eum habēre cupīvit; Claudius autem etiam puerum suum, Britannicum, habuit.

- Agrippina, because she loved her son Nero very much, wanted to have great power through him; Claudius, however, also had a son of his own, Britannicus.
- Main kernels: transitive with a complementary infinitive (*cupīvit habēre*, object *vīrēs magnās*); then transitive (*habuit*).
- **[amāns]**: pres. act., f. nom. sg., modifying the subject *Agrippīna*. Its object is *Nerōnem*, with *puerum suum* in apposition. Cause: "because she loved."
- *per eum*: accusative with a preposition; *eum* = Nero. *suum* in each half points back to that half's own subject (Agrippina, then Claudius).
- *puer* here means "son"; the lesson vocabulary has no *fīlius*.

**3.** {Agrippīnā saepe premente}, Claudius Nerōnem in domum suam accēpit.

- Since Agrippina kept pressing him, Claudius took Nero into his own house (that is, adopted him, AD 50).
- Main kernel: transitive. *in domum*: accusative with a preposition (place to which).
- **{Agrippīnā … premente}**: pres. act., f. abl. sg., with no object. Absolute: "Agrippina often pressing." Clause: "since Agrippina kept pressing (him)" (cause).

**4.** {Nerōne acceptō}, Britannicus, puer parvus et inops, ā patre neglēctus est.

- Once Nero had been taken in, Britannicus, a small and helpless boy, was neglected by his father.
- Main kernel: passive. *ā patre*: ablative of personal agent. *puer parvus et inops*: in apposition to the subject.
- **{Nerōne acceptō}**: pf. pass., m. abl. sg. The perfect participle shows the action came first. Absolute: "Nero having been taken in." Clause: "once Nero had been taken in" (time; cause is also possible).

**5.** tum Agrippīna, [avāritiā ducta], {venēnō in vīnum fūsō}, Claudium interfēcit.

- Then Agrippina, led by greed, poured poison into wine and killed Claudius (AD 54).
- Main kernel: transitive.
- **[ducta]**: pf. pass., f. nom. sg., modifying the subject. *avāritiā*: ablative of means. Cause: "because she was led by greed."
- **{venēnō … fūsō}**: pf. pass., n. abl. sg. Absolute: "poison having been poured into the wine." Clause: "after poison had been poured into the wine" (time, with a sense of means). English usually makes it active ("she poured poison… and killed him"), because Agrippina is clearly the one who poured it.
- Tacitus and Suetonius say the poison was in a dish of mushrooms; the story uses wine because the vocabulary has *vīnum*.

**6.** {Claudiō interfectō}, Nerō, [ā mātre adiūtus], Rōmam regere incēpit.

- After Claudius had been killed, Nero, since he was helped by his mother, began to rule Rome.
- Main kernel: transitive with a complementary infinitive (*incēpit regere*, object *Rōmam*).
- **{Claudiō interfectō}**: pf. pass., m. abl. sg. Absolute: "Claudius having been killed." Clause: "after Claudius had been killed" (time).
- **[adiūtus]**: pf. pass., m. nom. sg., modifying the subject. *ā mātre*: ablative of personal agent inside the participial clause. Cause: "since he was helped by his mother."

**7.** prīmum, {Nerōne mātrem audiente}, Agrippīna magnās vīrēs habuit et multa in urbe ēgit.

- At first, since Nero listened to his mother, Agrippina had great power and did many things in the city.
- Main kernels: transitive ×2 (*habuit* and *ēgit*). *multa*: n. acc. pl. used as a noun, "many things."
- **{Nerōne … audiente}**: pres. act., m. abl. sg., with its own object *mātrem*. Absolute: "Nero listening to his mother." Clause: "since Nero listened to his mother" (cause) or "while Nero listened to his mother" (time). Both are defensible.

**8.** mox autem, {mātre omnia agente}, Nerō īrā affectus est, et Agrippīna ab eō ē domō missa est.

- Soon, however, since his mother was running everything, Nero became angry, and Agrippina was sent out of the house by him (AD 55).
- Main kernels: passive ×2. *īrā*: ablative of means ("affected by anger" = became angry). *ab eō*: ablative of personal agent; *eō* = Nero. *ē domō*: ablative with a preposition (place from which).
- **{mātre omnia agente}**: pres. act., f. abl. sg., with its own object *omnia* (n. acc. pl., "everything"). Absolute: "his mother doing everything." Clause: "since his mother was running everything" (cause).
- *affectus est* is a finite passive verb (participle + *est*), not a participial clause. Compare *affectus* in 15, where there is no *est*.

**9.** Agrippīna, [hāc rē irrītāta], Nerōnem verbīs malīs pressit et Britannicum adiuvāre incēpit.

- Agrippina, provoked by this, pressed Nero with harsh words and began to help Britannicus.
- Main kernels: transitive (*pressit*); then transitive with a complementary infinitive (*incēpit adiuvāre*, object *Britannicum*). *verbīs malīs*: ablative of means.
- **[irrītāta]**: pf. pass., f. nom. sg., modifying the subject. *hāc rē*: ablative of cause/means; *hāc* is f. abl. sg. of *hic*, used as an adjective. Cause: "because she was provoked by this."
- Contrast *hāc rē cognitā* in 15: there *rē* is the noun of an ablative absolute; here it only goes with *irrītāta*.

**10.** Nerō, [magnum perīculum in frātre vidēns], eum etiam venēnō interfēcit.

- Nero, because he saw great danger in his brother, killed him too with poison.
- Main kernel: transitive. *venēnō*: ablative of means. *eum* = Britannicus (Nero's adoptive brother). *etiam*: "him too," as Claudius had been.
- **[vidēns]**: pres. act., m. nom. sg., modifying the subject, with its own object *perīculum*. Cause: "because he saw."

**11.** {venēnō Britannicum ante multōs hominēs statim vincente}, Agrippīna, [vēritātem cognōscēns], intus multum doluit.

- As the poison quickly overcame Britannicus in front of many people, Agrippina, recognizing the truth, grieved greatly inside.
- Main kernel: intransitive (*doluit*). *intus*: "inwardly." Tacitus says she hid her terror; the other guests saw nothing on her face.
- **{venēnō … vincente}**: pres. act., n. abl. sg. Here the poison is the subject of the clause and *Britannicum* is its object; *ante multōs hominēs* is an accusative with a preposition inside the clause. Absolute: "the poison overcoming Britannicus at once before many people." Clause: "as the poison quickly overcame Britannicus…" (time).
- Note that *venēnō* is the subject of the ablative absolute, not an ablative of means as in 10. The participle in agreement (*vincente*) shows this.
- **[cognōscēns]**: pres. act., f. nom. sg., modifying the subject, with its own object *vēritātem*. Time or cause: "as/because she recognized the truth."

**12.** post quattuor annōs Nerō, [mātrem interficere cupiēns], eam ad mare vocāvit.

- Four years later (AD 59), Nero, since he wanted to kill his mother, invited her to the sea.
- Main kernel: transitive. *post quattuor annōs*: accusative with a preposition (time). *ad mare*: place to which (Baiae, on the Bay of Naples).
- **[cupiēns]**: pres. act., m. nom. sg., modifying the subject. It takes a complementary infinitive (*interficere*) with its own object (*mātrem*). Cause: "since he wanted."

**13.** nocte, {stēllīs lūcem dantibus}, Agrippīna ā virīs [ā Nerōne missīs] in mare iacta est.

- At night, while the stars were giving light, Agrippina was thrown into the sea by men who had been sent by Nero.
- Main kernel: passive. *nocte*: ablative of time when. *ā virīs*: ablative of personal agent. *in mare*: accusative with a preposition (place to which).
- **{stēllīs lūcem dantibus}**: pres. act., f. abl. pl., with its own object *lūcem*. Absolute: "the stars giving light." Clause: "while the stars were giving light" (time). *dantibus* looks like a dative plural but is ablative here. Tacitus remarks that the gods granted a clear, starry night, as if to expose the crime.
- **[missīs]**: pf. pass., m. abl. pl., modifying *virīs*. The noun is ablative, but this is **not** an ablative absolute, because *virīs* already has a job in the main clause (the agent). *ā Nerōne*: a second agent, inside the participial clause. Attributive: "men (who had been) sent by Nero."
- The story simplifies the event. In Tacitus a boat built to collapse broke apart and threw her into the water; the vocabulary has no word for "ship" yet.

**14.** Agrippīna autem, [marī nōn victa], ex aquā fortiter fūgit et, {nocte vānēscente}, domum vēnit.

- Agrippina, however, since she was not overcome by the sea, bravely escaped from the water and, as the night was fading, came home.
- Main kernels: intransitive ×2 (*fūgit*, *vēnit*). *ex aquā*: place from which. *domum*: accusative of place to which without a preposition (Lesson 12).
- **[victa]**: pf. pass., f. nom. sg., modifying the subject. *marī*: ablative of means (a thing, so no *ā*). Cause or circumstance: "not overcome by the sea."
- **{nocte vānēscente}**: pres. act., f. abl. sg. Absolute: "the night fading." Clause: "as the night was fading" (time). Compare *nocte* alone in 13 (time when) with *nocte vānēscente* here: the participle makes it a clause.

**15.** {hāc rē cognitā}, Nerō, [magnā īrā affectus], virōs crūdēlēs ad mātrem mīsit.

- When this became known, Nero, gripped by great anger, sent cruel men to his mother.
- Main kernel: transitive. *ad mātrem*: place to which.
- **{hāc rē cognitā}**: pf. pass., f. abl. sg. Absolute: "this thing having been learned." Clause: "when this became known" (time). This is a set phrase; compare *quō factō*.
- **[affectus]**: pf. pass., m. nom. sg., modifying the subject; no *est*, so it is not a finite verb (compare 8). *magnā īrā*: ablative of means. Cause.

**16.** {virīs domum intrantibus}, Agrippīna, nōn timida sed fortis, statim interfecta est.

- As the men were entering the house, Agrippina, not timid but brave, was killed at once.
- Main kernel: passive. *nōn timida sed fortis*: adjectives agreeing with the subject, describing her as she died.
- **{virīs … intrantibus}**: pres. act., m. abl. pl., with its own object *domum* (*intrō* is transitive). Absolute: "the men entering the house." Clause: "as the men were entering the house" (time).

**17.** Nerō, {mātre sepultā}, novem annōs Rōmam rēxit; sed saepe nocte, [ē somnō excitātus], mātrem [facēs in manibus tenentem] vīdit.

- After his mother was buried, Nero ruled Rome for nine years (AD 59–68); but often at night, when he had been woken from sleep, he saw his mother holding torches in her hands.
- Main kernels: transitive ×2. *novem annōs*: accusative of extent of time without a preposition (Lesson 12). *nocte*: ablative of time when.
- **{mātre sepultā}**: pf. pass., f. abl. sg. Absolute: "his mother buried." Clause: "after his mother had been buried" (time). It sits inside the main clause, between subject and verb, but it is still adverbial.
- **[excitātus]**: pf. pass., m. nom. sg., modifying the subject. *ē somnō*: place from which. Time: "when he had been woken."
- **[tenentem]**: pres. act., f. acc. sg., modifying the object *mātrem*, with its own object *facēs*. With a verb of seeing, translate "saw his mother holding…" (as *mātrem dormientem vīdit* in the Lesson 14 story).
- Suetonius (*Nero* 34) says Nero admitted he was haunted by his mother's ghost and by the Furies with whips and blazing torches.

### Rule of thumb

Present participle: its action is happening at the same time as the main verb ("while,"
"as"). Perfect participle: its action happened before ("after," "once," "when … had").
